Curry Leaves AI Assistant · User Guide

Your second brain — listens, remembers, gets it done.

Curry Leaves turns every conversation into summaries, tasks, and knowledge that compounds — with a team of assistants you hire and schedule, all private and on your machine.

🎙 Transcribe 📝 Summarize ✅ Extract tasks 📚 Knowledge Hub 🎯 Today's Focus

Stop taking minutes, chasing action items, and redoing the same weekly chores. Curry Leaves listens to every conversation and turns it into summaries, tasks, and a memory that gets sharper the more you use it — then a team of assistants you design and schedule quietly runs your recurring work. All of it private, on your own machine.

A day with it

  • 9:00 — standup. ⌘R on the way in. You talk; nobody takes minutes. By 9:20 the summary is on the recording and two "I'll handle it" moments are todos with your name on them.
  • 11:30 — client call. The call ends; the follow-up draft is already an output on the recording. Ninety seconds of editing instead of twenty minutes of writing.
  • 14:00 — "didn't we settle this in March?" One search in Memory — by meaning, not keywords. The note says yes; the provenance link plays the exact sentence.
  • 16:30 — wrap-up. One glance at the Dashboard: what's still open, what tomorrow needs, what the assistants filed today. No tab archaeology.
  • Friday. The weekly-review assistant posts its recap on schedule, and Usage shows what the week of AI help actually cost.

And it compounds

A recorder helps you once; Curry Leaves gets more useful the longer you use it.

  • Memory grows richer, not messier. Every meeting and document distills into linked notes with provenance. By month three, asking Memory beats searching your drive — and the Gardener keeps it healthy while contradictions get flagged for your review, never silently overwritten.
  • It gets to know you. Each night an assistant re-reads the day's conversations and keeps only what's durable — how you work, what you're responsible for, what happened worth remembering. You stop re-explaining yourself.
  • The assistants are yours to shape. Nothing here is a fixed feature. Describe any workflow in plain English — "watch for customer complaints and draft an apology", "turn interviews into candidate scorecards" — and it becomes an assistant with exactly the tools, triggers, and permissions you chose.
  • Life admin runs on a schedule. A Monday-morning planning brief, a daily digest tile, a weekly review — scheduled assistants do the recurring work without being asked, and every run is traceable if you ever wonder what happened.

Overview

Everything in Curry Leaves flows from one idea: capture once, let assistants do the rest. When a recording finishes, it becomes an event; assistants that subscribe to that event wake up and work.

record→ transcribe on-device→ event→ assistants run→ summary · todos · knowledge

What makes it different:

  • Local-first. Audio never leaves your machine. Three models run on-device: Whisper for transcription, a small embedding model for search-by-meaning, and Kokoro for the voice that speaks back. Only the text your assistants work on goes to the AI provider you chose — or nowhere at all, with Ollama.
  • Files you own. Assistants are markdown. Notes are markdown. Settings are JSON. It all lives in ~/.curry-leaves — back it up, sync it, grep it.
  • Transparent. Every run is traced end-to-end and every token is metered, right down to what each assistant costs you per week.

Getting started

Run it

pip (fastest to try):

pip install curry-leaves-assistant
curry-leaves-assistant                    # then open http://localhost:5177

Docker (browser, no install):

cp src/curry_leaves_assistant/.env.docker.example .env   # add an API key, or skip for Copilot login
docker compose up --build                                # then open http://localhost:5177

Desktop app (from source):

./start.sh          # sets up the venv + node deps and launches Electron

Needs Python 3.11+. Spoken replies additionally need the system espeak-ng binary (brew install espeak-ng on macOS) — it's already in the Docker image.

First launch

A short wizard walks you through the setup, adapting to how you say you'll use it. Only the last step is required — everything else has a Skip, and you can change all of it later in Settings.

  1. Your name — what your assistants should call you.
  2. How you'll use it — meetings & recordings, voice assistant, notes & knowledge, a team of assistants. Not cosmetic: your picks go into your profile, shape what every assistant knows about the job it's doing for you, and decide which of the next steps you even see.
  3. Language for transcription.
  4. Transcription model — one is marked recommended based on step 2. It downloads in the background rather than blocking you.
  5. Voice (if you picked voice) — the speaking voice for hands-free replies.
  6. Knowledge (if you picked notes) — turn on search-by-meaning for your memory.
  7. AI provider — paste a key for any of a dozen services, sign in with GitHub Copilot or OpenAI Codex, point at a local Ollama, or add your own OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Keys are checked as you connect, so a bad one is refused rather than quietly saved.
  8. Create a PIN (4–12 digits). No account, no sign-up — this is your passcode, and setting it is what finishes setup.

Quit halfway and it resumes where you left off, with what you'd already entered. Then open Capture and talk — summary, action items and notes appear on the recording within a minute or two.

Running it headless? Set CURRY_LEAVES_PIN and the wizard is skipped entirely. And if the app can't reach its backend you get an editable Backend URL field rather than a spinner — which is also how a browser tab points at a backend running somewhere else.

What downloads on first use

Everything that runs locally fetches its weights once, in the background, so nothing blocks you:

  • Whisper — transcription. Pick the size in Settings → Recording.
  • MiniLM (~90 MB) — search by meaning. It warms in the background on first launch and switches on at your next start; until then search is keyword-only, and Memory shows a "keyword only" badge so you're never guessing.
  • Kokoro — the speaking voice, fetched the first time you use voice.
⌘R / Ctrl+R jumps to Capture and toggles recording from anywhere. ⌘K / Ctrl+K searches everything at once — notes, recordings, todos, reminders, artifacts, assistants — or type > for commands. ⌘⇧V / Ctrl+Shift+V talks to your assistant on the spot, even with the wake word switched off.

Everyday

Capture & Recordings

Capture

  • Record or Listen. Record saves automatically when you stop; Listen asks whether to keep or discard — handy for a quick check you don't want to file.
  • Live transcript appears as you speak (about every 8 seconds of audio), with pause/resume and a live level meter.
  • Meeting templates decide what you get when you stop — a "1:1" template might produce just a summary; "Client calls" adds a follow-up email draft. A template is an ordered list of output sections, and you can have Curry Leaves draft one for you from a sentence, then revise it.
  • Language & microphone — pick the input device and, per recording, the transcription language. Language follows your Settings default with a per-recording override, including auto-detect: twelve languages plus auto-detect.
  • Attachments, notes & attendees — drop the agenda or brief onto the recording (converted to text for context), pin timestamped notes as it runs, and add attendees; naming an attendee also pre-briefs the copilot and biases the transcriber toward their name.
  • Text or timeline — the toggle above the live transcript switches between one flowing passage and a timestamped list, oldest at the top, so you can see when something was said. Either way it follows the newest speech until you scroll up to read back.
  • Notes sit under the transcript — the note box and the running notes it pins into are together in the middle column, so you can see what you've jotted without leaving the conversation. Type @ to mention an attendee; it drops in their name.
  • Organizer — click the person icon on an attendee to mark whose meeting it is. Assistants treat them as the owner of any follow-up nobody else picked up.
  • Save to Knowledge Hub and Don't store audio are per-recording switches — file the meeting into Memory, and optionally discard the audio once the transcript exists.

A copilot in the room

If a meeting's template opts in, a live copilot watches the transcript while you're still talking and surfaces at most two small cards at a time — never a wall of text mid-conversation. A card might be an open loop nobody closed, an answer pulled out of your own notes, a question worth asking, or "you decided this in March". There's a refresh button if you want a pass right now.

Two assistants, two moments: the Live Copilot works during the meeting; the Meeting Copilot picks up after it ends, writing the template's sections and turning things people actually agreed to into todos or reminders.

Recordings

Every past recording with everything produced from it: the transcript, the summary and action items (tick them off in place), one output per section the template defines — each on its own tab — plus your own notes, links and tags, and a per-recording Save to Memory switch.

Rename a recording by clicking its title and typing; Enter saves, Escape puts it back. Renaming also stops the auto-titler from renaming it later, so a name you chose stays chosen.

Tags

Tag a recording while it's still running — the panel on the right of the Capture screen has a Tags row next to Attendees — or add them any time afterwards from Notes & files. Tags you've used before are offered as you type, so the same meeting kind keeps the same tag instead of drifting into standup, Standup and stand-up.

Turn on Group by tag above the list and recordings collapse into a group per tag, most-used first, with anything untagged at the bottom. A recording files under its first tag, so it shows up in exactly one place — the first tag is highlighted when you edit them, and clicking another one promotes it. Inside each group, the toggle beside it flips between newest and oldest first. Both choices are remembered.

Three ways to make it try again:

  • Re-run a single output section, or re-run the whole meeting copilot.
  • Fix & re-run — tweak the template in plain words ("add a risks section") and regenerate from that.
  • Resubmit — re-fire the recording so every assistant bound to it runs again from scratch.

Editing audio

Trim down to just the parts worth keeping: drag one or more regions of the waveform to keep, preview them, and save. The kept regions are stitched together and the recording is re-transcribed, which re-runs the whole pipeline on the new audio.

Dictation

Any text box with a microphone icon takes speech — short clips are transcribed straight into the field.

Everyday

Ask AI & Voice

A full chat workspace where you talk to any of your assistants — each with its own instructions, tools, and permissions. Conversations save automatically as sessions; open pages keep their state, so switching tabs never resets a chat.

  • Streaming replies with expandable thinking blocks, and a thinking-effort chip (off → high) when you want it to reason harder or faster.
  • Tool call cards show every tool the assistant used — inputs and results, sub-agents shown as a tree. Nothing happens off-screen.
  • Approvals. Before an assistant uses a tool that writes or acts, it pauses and asks — allow once, allow for this chat, always allow, or deny. Flip to Autonomous to let a run act without stopping, or leave it on Ask first. Assistants can ask you clarifying questions too.
  • Add while it works. Type mid-stream and choose to steer the current turn or queue a follow-up for after it lands; queued messages show as chips you can remove.
  • @-mention & attach. Pull a recording, note, todo, reminder or file into the prompt with @ (searched by meaning), or drop in files — PDFs are OCR'd — as context.
  • Pick the assistant and model per conversation, and toggle Smart context to reclaim tokens from stale tool results automatically.
  • Fork & save. Branch a new chat from any of your earlier messages, or turn a whole conversation into a reusable skill.
  • Compact. A context bar shows how full the window is; one click summarizes the chat into a recap and continues — same context, far fewer tokens.
A live chat outlives tab switches and can be watched read-only from another tab or device — you'll see a "Live" badge when a conversation is streaming somewhere else.

Talking to it

A Voice button in the composer does two things — and like transcription, the speech is generated on your machine. Nothing is sent away to be spoken.

  • Spoken replies. The assistant reads its answers aloud, starting on the first sentence rather than waiting for the whole reply to finish — so there's very little dead air.
  • Hands-free voice chat. Turn it on and just talk. It listens, notices when you've stopped (about 1.2 seconds of silence), sends your turn, speaks the reply, and goes back to listening. No buttons between turns.
One honest limitation: you can't talk over it. While the assistant is speaking, the microphone is off — deliberately, so it never transcribes its own voice. If it's heading off on a tangent, click the toggle to stop it and carry on.

"Hey Curry" — from anywhere in the app

That's voice inside the chat screen. There's also a wake word listening across the whole app, so you can ask something without stopping what you're doing. Say the phrase, a panel appears, you ask, it answers — out loud and on screen. One question per wake.

The listening happens inside the app itself. No audio goes anywhere to be recognised — not to a cloud service, not even to your own backend. The honest trade-off: the microphone really is open the whole time it's armed, so your system's mic indicator stays lit. The dot in the sidebar is there so that's never a mystery.

  • Six phrases built in — Hey Curry, Hey Assistant, Hey Buddy, Hey Dude, Hey Clone, Buddy Boy — one model tuned to recognise them all.
  • Or train your own. Drop a fused openWakeWord .onnx file into ~/.curry-leaves/models/wakeword/ and it appears in the picker.
  • Silent mode. Turn speech off and answers just appear on screen — useful in a shared room, and it works on installs with no speech output at all.
It answers; it doesn't act. A spoken question runs without the ability to stop and ask your permission, so anything needing approval won't go through. Ask it things by voice; use chat to have it do things. Spoken answers also aren't kept in your chat history — they're one-offs.

Off until you switch it on, and the detection model is a ~3 MB download. The built-in model is Curry Leaves' own, published on Hugging Face under Apache-2.0 — free to use, including commercially.

Everyday

Tasks

Everything actionable in one place.

  • Todos — with priority and due date. Assistants create them too: "I'll send the deck by Friday" in a meeting becomes a todo with a backlink to the recording it came from.
  • Reminders — time-anchored, with notes.
  • The pool — a shared queue between you and your team, and the front door for "here's a job, someone deal with it". Post a task (⌘K → New task) and the Lead assistant reads it, checks who's idle, and routes it to the best fit. The result lands back on the item.
The Lead routes; it doesn't do the work itself. It won't hire someone new for a task nobody fits, and it won't split one task across several assistants — it'll tell you instead.

Todos that start themselves

You don't have to hand every job to the team by hand. The moment you add a todo, a built-in Todo Triage assistant reads it and makes one call: can the team actually do this with their tools?

  • Actionable work — "research our top 3 competitors", "summarize the Q3 notes", "draft a dashboard tile for open risks" — is posted straight to the pool. The Lead routes it to the best-fit assistant, and the todo flips to Working while the run is in flight.
  • Personal reminders — "call mom", "buy milk", "take medication" — are left completely alone. Those are yours to do; the team never touches them.

When the assistant finishes, the result is attached back onto the todo and it moves to Review — so the answer is waiting on the item itself, for now and for later. The run's whole conversation is kept too, so you can open it and continue where it left off if you want more. Nothing is lost, and nothing loops: the write-back never re-triggers triage.

Triage only judges and hands off — it never does the task itself, never edits your todo, and when it's unsure it leaves the todo alone rather than spending a run on a maybe. It's a built-in assistant like any other, so you can inspect or disable it under Assistants.

Everyday

Dashboard

Your home tab — always open, always live. Boards hold tiles, and every tile is a standing question you've given an assistant: "Unresolved action items this week", "What needs prep tomorrow?". Each refresh, the assistant runs and the tile shows the answer — in a checked, structured shape, which is why tiles stay clean instead of drifting into stray prose. A general-purpose assistant ships ready for this, so you can add your first tile without building anything first.

  • Focus & rules — the tile's brief, plus optional constraints (length, filters, tone).
  • Output formats — summary, list, metric, table, markdown, or diff; plus ready-made shapes like Briefing doc and Meeting brief that give the answer a fixed skeleton.
  • Styles — card, flat, outlined, accent-bar, and three fancy ones: glass, glow, gradient-edge; all theme-aware.
  • Refresh — manual, on a schedule (down to a cron expression), or on an event (e.g. every new transcript).
  • Alerts — give a tile a condition and it can fire a desktop notification when the answer meets it.
  • AI setup — describe what the tile should show in one sentence and Curry Leaves drafts the title, focus, rules, best-fit format, and empty-state message.

Two ways to view it: an Inbox that reads like a briefing, or classic Boards of tiles you drag and resize — layouts save automatically, and you can create as many boards as you like.

Your second brain

Memory & Skills

One screen for everything remembered: your notes (people, projects, decisions), what your assistants have learned about you, what they've learned about their own work, and their skills — the procedures for how things get done.

All of it is one bundle of plain markdown notes on disk, sorted by what kind of thing each note is rather than filed into separate folders. That's not an implementation detail you can ignore: it's what lets Curry Leaves connect a fact back to the meeting that produced it, and lets a single search span your notes, what it knows about you, and what happened last month — all at once.

Your notes

  • Notes are plain markdown with wiki-style [[links]]. Browse the tree and edit in place with a proper rich editor — you see headings, tables and lists as they'll look, while what's on disk stays plain markdown any other app can open.
  • Tables — Tab moves across cells and Enter down a row, so you can fill a column without reaching for the mouse. For a second line inside one cell, press Shift+Enter. Drag a column or row border to resize it; the sizes are remembered and the note still reads as an ordinary markdown table anywhere else.
  • Twelve kinds of live block — flowcharts (Mermaid), charts, diff charts, timelines, calendars, kanban boards, mind maps, waterfalls, sankey flows, API specs, drawings and tabbed sections. Insert one from the editor and it renders interactively, but stays editable as plain text underneath so nothing is trapped in a widget. They render everywhere the app shows markdown, so a chart an assistant draws also appears properly in chat replies, meeting summaries and dashboard tiles.
  • Drawings — pick Insert → Drawing for a freehand canvas (boxes, arrows, text, hand-drawn sketching) right inside a note, in the spirit of Excalidraw or draw.io. It runs entirely on your machine — nothing is uploaded, and the canvas makes no network requests at all. The drawing is saved into the note as plain readable JSON, so it travels with the note and stays diffable rather than becoming an opaque attachment. Drag the drawing's bottom edge to make it taller or shorter (double-click that edge to reset it), or use the align and ± buttons in its header. For serious drawing, hit ⛶ in the header to take over the whole window — press Esc to come back. Reading a note, you can click any drawing to see it full screen the same way. One limit worth knowing: pictures pasted into a drawing aren't kept — add the image to the note itself instead, and the drawing will tell you if you paste one.
  • AI editing, two ways. Ask from the cursor and you get a compact accept/reject card. Select a passage first and you get a full diff of the note instead, hunk by hunk — so you can see everything a rewrite touched beyond the part you highlighted, and take only what you want.
  • Keep a conversation going with the AI. While editing, the ✦ AI button in the note's header opens a panel on the right where you can ask for a change and then keep talking — the AI remembers the earlier turns, so "now make that shorter" or "put the summary at the top instead" simply works. Every change is still reviewed as the usual diff before anything lands, and the panel stays beside it so you can reply while a proposal is on screen. Each turn leaves a line in the thread saying what happened ("Applied 2 changes", "Kept 1 of 3"), and if you close a proposal without deciding you can reopen it from that line. Clear starts a fresh conversation; closing the panel keeps it. Remember the AI only changes your working copy — press Save to keep the result.
  • AI on a table, block or image. Hover one and a ✦ AI button appears at its corner, with actions that suit it — fill in missing cells, add a summary row or sort a table; add items to a board; write alt text for an image — or just describe the change. Only that element is rewritten, and you review it as a diff first.
  • Search by meaning, not just matching words — ask "what did we decide about pricing" and get the note that says it, however it was phrased. This needs a small model that downloads in the background on first launch and switches on at your next start; until then search is keyword-only and says so.
  • Graph view maps the whole base — clusters and orphans at a glance.
  • History keeps every version of every note, with restore and a note of what changed it.
  • Provenance links what a note claims to the exact transcript span it came from — speaker and timecodes included.
  • Conflicts — contradicting information flags the note for your review instead of silently overwriting it, with a count in the tree so you can find them.
  • Archive — retire a note without deleting it; it moves aside but stays searchable.

What it remembers

A Facts tab sorts everything remembered into four plain-language kinds, with one search box across all of them:

KindWhat it holds
FactsWhat every assistant knows about you — how you work, what you're responsible for
Assistant notesHow one assistant does its own job. Private to it
EventsNotable things that actually happened, worth referring back to
LessonsDurable takeaways folded out of clusters of past events

You can add a fact yourself, and you can make it forget anything — nothing here is a black box you're stuck with.

Feeding it

Recordings with Save to Memory file themselves. To add outside material, use the Ingest tab (Feed docs): paste text or upload documents — PDF, Word, PowerPoint, Excel and more are converted to markdown, and scans are OCR'd. The Activity panel shows every job live: converting → queued → running → filed, and the filer edits an existing note rather than making a duplicate when the material already exists.

Keeping it healthy

Health is a read-only sweep — broken links, orphans, missing frontmatter, tag drift. The Gardener is the maintenance pass: it repairs dangling links, flags stale conflicts and aging notes, and writes a report you can read after every pass.

Skills

Reusable playbooks stored as folders — a SKILL.md plus references, scripts, assets. Edit a skill's whole file tree in place, create new skills from the UI, and bind them to assistants. A built-in skill-learner grows new ones from what it observes.

Your second brain

How it gets to know you

The thing that makes an assistant useful on day ninety rather than day one is that it stopped needing to be told. Curry Leaves works at this deliberately, and in the open.

It reads the day back

Each night, an assistant called the Memory Keeper re-reads the conversations you had that day — the clean text of them, with tool calls and approvals stripped out — and learns only from what you said. It keeps two kinds of thing: durable facts about you, and events worth remembering.

The bar is set high on purpose. A profile full of trivia is worse than a sparse one, so most of a conversation is correctly thrown away. A chat is only re-read if it's grown since last time.

It learns how to work, too

Separately, the Skill Learner watches how runs actually go. When something is repeatedly done the slow way or fails the same way twice, it writes it up as a trial skill — on probation, not law. Skills that demonstrably help get promoted; ones that don't get retired. Nothing is permanent because it happened once.

You can see and edit all of it

Everything above lands in Memory → Facts, in plain language, with a Forget button. There is no hidden profile. If it learned something wrong, delete it; if it missed something, add it yourself.

Assistants can also search their own memory mid-task, by meaning — so they can reach for something relevant that wasn't already loaded into the conversation.

Everyday

Artifacts

Most of what an assistant makes is text in a chat. Artifacts are for the things that aren't — a deck, a report, a one-page site, a diagram. Ask for "a short deck on last quarter's retro" and it's written as a self-contained page and saved here.

  • A real link. Every artifact gets a shareable URL with an unguessable token — send it to someone with no Curry Leaves, no account, no login. They can open it and download it.
  • Revocable. Changed your mind? Revoke link mints a new token, and every URL you shared before stops working immediately.
  • Editable. Text artifacts can be edited in place; Open shows you the exact page a recipient sees, so there's only ever one rendering to trust.

They live in ~/.curry-leaves/artifacts/, are included in backups, and turn up in ⌘K search like everything else.

Your second brain

Assistants, Tools & MCP

Assistants are the workforce. A team ships with the app — a general chat assistant, a lead that routes incoming work, a live meeting copilot and a post-meeting one, a filer and a maintainer for your notes, a triage assistant that turns actionable todos into real work automatically, plus two that quietly learn (one about you, one about how the work goes) — and you can hire your own in a couple of minutes. The Assistants screen has five tabs: List, Team, Tools, MCP, and an experimental Office.

The List — what's happening right now

The default view is three live columns: your assistants grouped by state (working, scheduled, idle, paused); the Work Kernel showing every job running, queued, or given up on; and the common pool, where you post a job and the Lead routes it to the best-fit assistant. It's the fastest way to see what the team is doing and what's waiting on you.

The Team

A roster of staff cards: who they are, what they do, whether they're working right now, when they last ran, their shift in plain words ("Every day at 09:00") — and what each one has cost you over the last week, down to the cent. Filter to Everyone, just Yours, or the Default team.

Hiring

Describe the role, or tap one of the ready-made shapes (News Scout, Inbox Watcher, Note Gardener, Meeting Sidekick, Daily Planner, Research Analyst). Curry Leaves drafts three different candidates at once — a focused one, a thorough one, an on-call one — and you hire whichever fits, or open one up and tweak it before saving.

You can still build one by hand: instructions, tools, triggers, schedule, model, step and permission limits. Assistants live as markdown under ~/.curry-leaves/agents/ — read them, diff them, share them.

When they run

On triggers (new transcript, document ingested, todo created…), on a schedule (including cron expressions), manually, from the pool, or in chat. Work is queued properly — assistants that write to your notes never trample each other, a crashed job retries and is eventually set aside rather than looping forever, and one waiting on your approval steps out of the queue instead of blocking it. The List view shows all of that live: what's running, what's queued, what gave up.

Tools & MCP

The Tools tab lists everything assistants can do, each with a risk level and global on/off. The MCP tab extends the toolbox with any MCP server — a local command (stdio) or a remote HTTP endpoint: test the connection, pick exactly which tools to expose, and they appear in every assistant's tool list.

The Office Coming soon

An experimental view — turned off in the current build — renders the team as an isometric office floor: each assistant a person at a desk who walks to the room that matches the tool they pick up (the library to read your notes, the web desk to search, the studio to make something), with a front desk where work queues and your desk where anything awaiting your approval waits. Until it's switched on, the List is home.

Under the hood

Trace & Usage

Curry Leaves never does anything you can't inspect.

Trace Beta

Every piece of work is a tree of spans — the triggering event, each LLM turn, every tool call, any sub-agents — with timing, tokens, and status. Click a span to see exactly what was sent to the model: the system prompt, your input, the tools, skills and deferred tools it had, the full conversation, and the output. Why did this todo appear? Why was that run slow? Follow the chain and see.

Usage Beta

A durable, never-pruned ledger of every AI call — totals and breakdowns by model, assistant, surface, and day, with tokens (including cache reads and writes) and estimated cost, over 7 / 30 / 90 days or all time. What each assistant costs you per week is one tab away — the same figure that shows on their card in the Team roster.

Pick the active provider and default model — model lists are fetched live from the provider, and your key is checked when you connect, so a wrong key is refused on the spot instead of failing later mid-run. Assistants can override the provider and model; chats can override the model per-conversation.

Each provider can also map four optional effort tiers — Less, Medium, Heavy, Smart — to different models, so routine work runs cheap and hard work runs on something stronger.

General

Turn on Verbose activity to show every tool call and sub-agent in chat rather than just thinking, control desktop notifications, and browse the full list of keyboard shortcuts. A system-info panel shows your version, engine, data directory, and the backend URL the app is talking to.

Recording

Choose a Whisper model size (tiny → large-v3 — bigger is more accurate, slower to download), a default language (12 built in, plus auto-detect), and a custom vocabulary of names and jargon the transcriber should recognize. Apple Silicon uses a fast native backend; everything else falls back to a cross-platform one automatically.

Wake word

Switch on the app-wide voice assistant, choose the phrase and how sensitive it is, and decide whether answers are spoken or silent, which voice reads them, and how long the answer panel stays up. You can also keep listening after each answer for back-to-back questions, tune the silence gap before your turn is sent, and pick whether work it wants to queue is confirmed out loud or just queued. Changes apply immediately. Off by default — the detection models are a small download first.

Meeting templates

Build and manage the templates that decide what a finished recording produces — and, optionally, what the live copilot watches for while you're still talking.

Identity

Tell your assistants who they work for — name, what you do, how you like them to behave, working hours. Every assistant gets this context, so outputs fit you without repeating yourself.

Appearance & security

Light, dark, or follow the system, plus a theme picker — the whole UI is token-driven, tiles included. The app locks behind your PIN (lock any time from the sidebar), and the only network calls are to the AI provider you chose and any MCP servers you added.

Backup & export

"Your data is plain files you own" only means something if you can pick them up and walk. So: Export gives you everything as a single zip, and Restore takes that zip back — on this machine or a new one.

Settings → Backup & data also shows where your files actually live and what's taking up the space, broken down by category.

  • Model weights and the live job queue are left out of the backup — weights just re-download, and a half-finished queue isn't something you want to carry to another machine. Both are preserved as-is when you restore, so nothing in flight gets clobbered.
  • A zip that isn't a Curry Leaves backup is rejected before anything is touched. Restore checks first, then acts.

FAQ & troubleshooting

Is my audio sent to the cloud?
No. Three things run entirely on your machine: transcription (Whisper), search by meaning (a small embedding model), and the voice that speaks back (Kokoro). Only the text your assistants work on goes to your chosen AI provider — and with Ollama, even that stays local.
Where exactly is my data?
~/.curry-leaves — settings, assistants, recordings (audio + transcripts), memory/ (your notes and everything remembered, as markdown), skills/, templates/, artifacts/, models/, chat sessions, events, run history. All plain markdown and JSON. In Docker, the same layout lives in the curry-leaves-data volume.
Can I edit things outside the app?
Yes — that's the point. Assistants and notes are markdown, settings are JSON. Edit them with anything; the app picks up changes.
The first transcription is slow
The Whisper model downloads once, on first use. You can pre-download models in Settings → Recording, or pick a smaller size.
Search isn't finding things by meaning yet
The ~90 MB model that powers it downloads quietly in the background on first launch and switches on at your next start — this is deliberate, so you never wait on a download in front of a query. Until then search is keyword-only, and Memory shows a "keyword only" badge. It never leaves your machine; set CURRY_LEAVES_VECTOR_SEARCH=0 to stay on keyword permanently.
The wake word doesn't respond
Check Settings → Wake word: is it enabled, and are the models downloaded? The toggle won't work until they are. If it's on but ignores you, lower the sensitivity; if it wakes at random noise, raise it. It's also deliberately deaf while an answer panel is on screen — dismiss the last answer first. Speech output isn't required: turn Speak the answer off and you still get answers on screen.
Why does my microphone indicator stay on?
Because the wake word is listening. Detecting the phrase happens entirely inside the app and no audio is sent anywhere, but the microphone genuinely is open while it's armed — that's what the dot in the sidebar is telling you. Turn the wake word off and the indicator goes out.
Voice doesn't work / there's no Voice button
Speech synthesis needs the system espeak-ng binary — brew install espeak-ng on macOS, or your package manager elsewhere (it's already in the Docker image). If the backend can't load it, the Voice button is hidden rather than shown broken. Restart after installing.
An assistant didn't run after my recording
Check the assistant is enabled with the right trigger (usually recording.transcribed), and check the recording's meeting template — the template decides what gets produced. The Trace screen shows exactly which assistants were triggered and what happened.
Chat errors, or the provider returns nothing
Settings → AI providers: is it connected, is the key valid, is a model selected? Keys are validated when you connect, so a bad one usually fails there rather than here. The Usage screen confirms whether calls are going through.
How do I back up, or move to a new machine?
Settings → Backup & data → Export for a single zip of everything, then Restore it on the new machine. Model weights are excluded (they re-download) and the live job queue is left alone.
How do I find something across everything?
⌘K (or Ctrl+K) searches notes, recordings, todos, reminders, artifacts and assistants at once. Type > instead to run a command.
I forgot my PIN
Delete the auth file inside ~/.curry-leaves (it holds only the PIN hash and tokens) and restart — the next PIN you enter becomes the new passcode. Your data is untouched.
